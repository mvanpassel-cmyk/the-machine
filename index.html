Hall I

The Nonchalance Machine

Designed 1528 · never used on purpose

A button with one function: it does not work when you use it. Nonchalance rises only while you touch nothing. Scrolling and reading are allowed. Clicking, typing and wanting are not.

The machine was built in the Netherlands and follows the national guideline: doe maar gewoon, dan doe je al gek genoeg. Just act normal; that's crazy enough already.

Nonchalance35
The machine is waiting. Not for you. Just in general.

Fine.

Aha · Hall I

In 1528 Baldassare Castiglione described sprezzatura: a certain nonchalance that conceals all art and makes everything look effortless. Courtiers spent years practising that effortlessness. You just reached it by doing nothing. That is the only route, and you cannot take it by trying.

Hall II

The Apathy Machine

Runs only while you watch

Seven concerns are slowly draining. You can care about them, but caring costs care units, and your reservoir refills more slowly than the concerns leak. It was calibrated that way on purpose. It is also calibrated that way in real life.

Scroll away and everything stops. Look back and it leaks again.

Choose what you care about. You cannot choose everything.

Aha · Hall II

To the Stoics, apatheia was a virtue: freedom from disturbing passions. Only later did it become a complaint. You just let something go, and the world kept turning.

Apathy is rarely a lack of feeling. It is a budget that does not balance. What you kept alive says more about you than what you let go.

Hall III

The Maslow's Pyramid Machine

Designed by someone else

Fill your needs, from the bottom up. The machine is strict: skipping a layer is not allowed. Beneath the pyramid lies a foundation that was added later. Everyone recognises it.

Self-actualisation waits at the top. What comes after is not in the drawing.

Start at the bottom. Everyone starts at the bottom.

Aha · Hall III

Abraham Maslow never drew this pyramid. The shape appeared in 1960, in the work of management consultant Charles McDermid, and was copied everywhere after that. Maslow himself wrote that the order is not rigid. This machine refused you anyway. That is how the pyramid works in practice: stricter than its author.

Late in life Maslow described one more level above self-actualisation: self-transcendence, committing yourself to something larger than yourself. It never made it into the drawing.

Hall IV

The Knowledge Machine

The more you know, the bigger it gets

What you know is an island. Each time you learn something, the island grows, and so does the coast where the known meets the unknown. Every fact produces a new question. The conversation never ends. That is what it is for.

Aha · Hall IV

The area of a circle grows with the square of its radius, the circumference only with the radius. But the coast is where the questions are, and it never runs out. Marcelo Gleiser called this the island of knowledge.

As for that line with the peak and the valley: it is not in Dunning and Kruger's 1999 study. It appeared later, on the internet. You recognised it anyway. That is knowledge too, just not the kind you thought you had.

Hall V

The Meta­machine

You are here · you were here all along

This machine does nothing itself. It watches the other machines, and above all you, and writes down what it sees. You are now reading what it writes about how you read what it writes.

It can also build machines. Even machines that build machines. There is a limit to that. The limit is not technical.
